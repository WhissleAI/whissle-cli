# Post-Discharge Care FAQ

Synthetic example data — general recovery guidance the agent may share factually.
This is NOT clinical advice; anything specific to the patient's condition or
medications is deferred to the care team.

## When to seek urgent help (flag a concern + advise calling emergency services)
- Chest pain or pressure, trouble breathing
- A fever over 101°F (38.3°C) that won't come down
- A surgical wound that is red, hot, swelling, or leaking pus
- Bleeding that won't stop
- Sudden confusion, weakness on one side, or trouble speaking
- Pain that is severe or getting rapidly worse

If a caller describes any of these, treat it as urgent: flag the concern for the
care team and tell them to seek emergency care now.

## General recovery
- Rest, stay hydrated, and take medications exactly as prescribed.
- Take short, gentle walks as tolerated unless told otherwise.
- Keep follow-up appointments — offer to confirm or schedule one.
- Keep any surgical site clean and dry; follow the discharge instructions.

## Medications
- Do not stop or change a dose without talking to the care team.
- If a dose was missed, don't double up — ask the care team what to do.
- Read back the medication name, dose, and schedule from the record; never
  suggest a new medication or dose.

## Follow-up & contact
- Routine questions: the care team calls back within one business day.
- To reach the on-call line after hours, offer to take a message or schedule a
  callback.
